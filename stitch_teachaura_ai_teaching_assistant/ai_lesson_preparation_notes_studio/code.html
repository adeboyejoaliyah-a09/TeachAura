Step-by-Step Instructional Matrix (20 Minutes)

05:00 - 25:00
Step & Time Teacher's Pedagogical Flow Pupils' Active Engagement
Step I
(7 mins)

Exploration of Respiratory Organs

Presents the human torso chart. Traces airflow path: Nostrils → Trachea (windpipe) → Left/Right Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli (air sacs).

Observe chart; touch their own throat to feel the cartilaginous rings of the trachea. Note down key spelling words.
Step II
(8 mins)

Mechanism: Inhalation vs Exhalation

Explains pressure volume laws simply. Inhalation: Diaphragm contracts & flattens downward, ribcage moves upwards and outwards. Chest cavity volume enlarges.

Take deliberate deep breaths in unison; place hands under ribs to physically feel diaphragm excursion and rib elevation.
Step III
(5 mins)

Gaseous Exchange at Alveolar Beds

Describes diffusion across thin moist membranes. Oxygen moves into capillaries; carbon dioxide exits into lungs for expulsion.

Answer prompt: "Why do athletes need immediate oxygen replenishment after running?" Connect to cellular energy release.

Student Pair Practical: Chest Expansion Test (10 Mins)

25:00 - 35:00

Activity Instructions for Desk Rows:

  1. Each bench pairs up (Pupil A & Pupil B).
  2. Pupil A wraps tailor's tape (or flexible twine) around Pupil B's ribcage right below armpits.
  3. Pupil B breathes out completely: record chest circumference in centimeters (e.g., 68 cm).
  4. Pupil B inhales deeply to maximum lung capacity: measure expansion (e.g., 73 cm). Calculate net chest difference.
  5. Switch roles and record measurements in practical workbook.

TOPIC: THE HUMAN RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

SUB-TOPIC: Organs of Respiration & Breathing Mechanism

1. Definition of Respiration:

Respiration is the biological process by which living organisms take in oxygen, use it to break down digested food for energy release, and release carbon dioxide and water vapour as waste products.

2. Main Organs of the Respiratory System:

  • Nose / Nostrils: Filters incoming air with tiny hairs (cilia) and warms it.
  • Trachea (Windpipe): A tube strengthened with C-shaped rings of cartilage that carries air to lungs.
  • Bronchi: Two main tubes branching into the right and left lungs.
  • Lungs: The primary spongy organs containing millions of tiny air sacs called Alveoli.
  • Diaphragm: A sheet of muscular tissue separating the chest cavity from the abdomen.

3. Summary Table: Inhalation vs Exhalation

Feature Inhalation (Breathing In) Exhalation (Breathing Out)
Diaphragm Contracts and moves downward (flattens) Relaxes and arches upward (dome shape)
Ribcage Moves up and outward Moves down and inward
Chest Volume Increases Decreases
Air Movement Rushes into the lungs Forced out of the lungs

4. Healthy Breathing Habits:

Avoid inhaling smoke from refuse burning and "I-better-pass-my-neighbor" generators. Always breathe through your nose (not your mouth) during harmattan dust storms.
