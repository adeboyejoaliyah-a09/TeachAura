Step 1: Anatomy Orientation Done

Point to the trachea and bronchi on the classroom poster chart. Ask students to place their flat hands firmly on the lower edge of their ribcage.

2
Step 2: Synchronized Kinesthetic Breathing Now Teaching

Count: “Breathe in... 1, 2, 3”. Guide synchronized deep breath: Notice how ribs move upwards and outwards while chest volume expands. Have them release: ribs descend, diaphragm relaxes up.

lightbulb

Pro-Tip for Crowded Classrooms: Have students turn to their bench partner on the wooden three-seater desks so every child can observe the chest movement profile sideways without standing up.

3
Step 3: Physical Analogy (Boyle's Law simplified)

Demonstrate the plastic bottle lung model (or draw syringe analogy) to show how pressure decreases when cavity volume increases.

Chalkboard View
1. INHALATION (Active)
  • External intercostal muscles contract
  • Ribs swing UP and OUT
  • Diaphragm contracts & flattens downward
  • Thoracic cavity expands → Air pressure drops
  • Atmospheric air rushes into lungs
2. EXHALATION (Passive)
  • External intercostals relax
  • Ribs drop DOWN and IN
  • Diaphragm relaxes into dome shape
  • Thoracic cavity shrinks → Air pressure rises
  • Air is forced outward through trachea
Teacher hint: Write these 2 parallel columns on blackboard right wing.

Teacher Question to Call Out

“If a person's phrenic nerve is damaged and the diaphragm cannot contract or flatten, what immediately happens to their breathing?”

verified Model Answer for Class:

They cannot create negative intra-thoracic pressure naturally. The thoracic cavity cannot expand downward, leading to severe shortness of breath or respiratory failure unless artificial ventilation is provided.

Common Nigerian Student Misconception: Students often assume lungs are active pumps that inflate like balloons on their own; remind them the lungs are passive sponge tissue moved by surrounding muscles!

Student Notebook Summary (Week 6)

Topic: Mechanism of Respiration

1. Respiration involves taking in oxygen (inhalation) and giving out carbon dioxide (exhalation).

2. During Inhalation: Diaphragm flattens, ribs move up/out, chest volume increases, lungs pull in air.

3. During Exhalation: Diaphragm curves upwards, ribs move down/in, chest volume decreases, air is pushed out.
